Biographical sketch

I am marine biogeochemist studying how distributions of trace metals impact life in the oceans. I will be completing my PhD in Earth Sciences at the University of Southern California at the end of August 2026.

My research interests include isotope geochemistry, atmospheric deposition modeling, oxidation and reduction of natrually occuring materials, application of synchrotron techniques, and impacts of global greenhouse gas emissions in the Anthropocene.

Research highlights

Natural iron fertilization of phytoplankton blooms

Annual summer phytoplankton blooms near the Hawaiian Islands draw down significant amounts of atmospheric carbon dioxide. We examine if pulses of atmospheric deposition containing Fe stimulates these blooms.

Aerosol iron supply and its influence on the ecology of the surface ocean

Atmospheric dust from lithogenic, biogenic, and anthropogenic sources supplies variable amounts of Fe to the surface oceans. We measured Fe in aerosols, rainwater, and surface seawater across multiple environmental gradients in the Pacific Ocean.

The environmental impact of urban wildfires

Urban wildfires rapidly propagate contaminants to the environment. We monitored the distribution of contaminants following the Eaton Fire in LA and their persistence in residential areas.

Elevated Environmental Lead Concentrations Following a Major Urban Fire

The LA wildfires devastated tens of thousands of homes, and mobilized pollutants across the greater Los Angeles Area in excess of healthy thresholds. Burning structures, especially older homes with lead based infrastructure, was the likely source of lead contamination.

Surface Water Iron Deposition Histories and the Initiation of Phytoplankton Blooms in the North Pacific Subtropical Gyre

We developed a novel modeling framework that integrates surface ocean circulation with aerosol chemistry to reconstruct the history of aerosol Fe deposition onto surface waters and evaluate whether episodic increases in Fe input stimulate phytoplankton blooms.
